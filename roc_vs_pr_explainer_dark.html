Why do these two curves tell such different stories?

Out of 259,335 test transactions, only 1,538 (0.6%) are actual fraud. The ROC curve's false-positive-rate axis divides by the entire legit population (257,797), so even thousands of false alarms barely move the needle as a percentage — the curve hugs the top-left corner almost the whole way across. The PR curve's precision axis divides only by what the model actually flagged, a much smaller number, so the same false alarms show up immediately as a drop in precision. That's why PR-AUC, not ROC-AUC, is the standard metric for rare-event problems like fraud detection.

Drag the slider to the far left: recall barely changes, but false positives explode — the real cost hidden behind an impressive-looking ROC-AUC score.
